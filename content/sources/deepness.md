Questions as Answers (QaA): Questions stimulate inquisitiveness and exploration of multiple possibilities, the "what-ifs" that unlock reasoning never considered otherwise. Questions, therefore, are triggers of intellectual work, and have psychological power by highlighting some potential "leak" or "void", which begets answers (and investigation, as humans focus more on what they lack or the negative than positive due to survival instincts). Always answers, in plurality, because there are always multiple paths, yet that does not mean they are right. A question prompts, then, prioritization, a search for the most effective, satisfying or overall superior option in an endless sea of branching conclusions, honed and trimmed for the very best.

Debates: Debates target engagement. Animosity brews in dynamics; one-on-one debates may have "attack" and "defend" dynamics, with ideas that counter each other. All those "swings" form and call attention to the act of expressing concepts due to the presentation; an engaging shock of perspectives. The association, contrast, and relationship of or between arguments feed the atmosphere of constant immersion in viewpoints, if this is true, what else is? What are the consequences? And the retaliation of the opposition.

Learning more in conversations: The potential force of infinite conceptual expansion.
LMIC: Learning in conversation builds connection. It is an active process of aggregation. One say something, the other says another. And these two specifically are connected. Unlike debates, it may not even have oppositions. Conversations are flexible. Unpredictable, as the endless recursive idea framework in one person is multiplied by as many people are in a conversation. These frames and concepts bounce off each other, building and connecting what they do not, in a cooperative reasoning process, cementing itself in novel ways.

Teaching as learning; how the support and teaching of one's mastering process boosts the mentor's own. Teaching as learning agrees on duty. The mentor is as chained as the mentored in a journey of iteration and improvement. Trial-and-error formed the mentor's experience and knowledge, now honed to be shareable, the hard-worked wisdom and tricks turned legacy, as now, too, the mentored inherits not only the information, but practices, frameworks, perhaps beyond, in the realms of philosophy, viewpoints that redefine their life, and the mentor too, as they must confront and revise what they have to share.

Memory, time, space, experience, life - The hardest, most impactful things are intangible.
Memory, time, space, experience, life reach infinity. They are mere names, vessels for something far more vast, never fully knowable, but the journey to hazard attempts to grasp it, or glimmers of understanding, always make the whole process worthwhile. Memory is record, history, fragmented, imperfect, personal, the traces of life. Time; in a deadline, the longer it passes, the less is left; the most precious resource that never comes back, and invites possibilities as one dreams of what would happen if it would. Space; once an insurmountable barrier, is more accessible now than before. Rendered useless in the realms of pure information.

Experience - The hard-fought, tears and sweat-earned grueling, practical spikes of memory, now guides for choices.
Life - The endless road, infinite in perception and possibility, limited by space and time, endless in potential experience and memory.

Transcendence - The dreams of perfection and the final destination of ultimate ideals
Transcendence - The known knowns, known unknown, unknown unknowns, and unknown knowns, all made known knowns. Transcendence embodies perfection, it is more of an ideal, a symbol than a state of being, as one could go mad or simply stop trying, turn apathetic, and lose the will and reason to experience life, now that they have it all. Transcendence is mystery. If we are imperfect, to aim for the impossible, for transcendence is another quest to try to reach somewhere we never will, but never stop trying. That is worth for it gives us the meaning and will to improve and enjoy life.

Knowledge - Knowledge is power, the opposite of what creates fear, and the closest to, or perhaps, perfection itself.

Knowledge is means. The tools and ways which we shape our environment, take choices, unknown unknowns, unknown knowns, known knowns, known unknowns, are the quadrant of perception. When one element in one quadrant moves to the other, our perception has been altered so vastly it may branch into all aspects of our life so strategy, efficiency is monumental. Meanwhile, prioritization of what works best is true knowledge.

Elite; Elite is unparalleled progress. Major refinement of known tools, techniques and know-how; honed expertise, applied in ways not considered before. Elite-like methodologies include framework; patterns to develop processes via efficient, state-of-the-art execution. That is achieved through clarity; defined goals and constraints, consistency, deadlines, and expected results, with a vision for the future and the consequences of this progress.

Literature: Literature is life in stories. Guides or warnings about how to live, the immersion in tales makes it a timeless experience, and how each person dives into them is another story in itself. The personal value stories provide is also remarkable, as even when one does not remember all of the logical details, and the sequence of facts and the plot, most can say they remember well how a story makes them feel.

Philosophy: Philosophy is meaning in life. It asks and voices perspectives, frameworks, ideals, and how to approach to anything in life. The legacy a philosophy leaves behind is among the most critical traits, for the search for meaning is a valid experience, but in matters large such as philosophy and life, made of multiple searches, value must be sought at its end too. A philosophy that has no, or worse, a negative value, is a life haunted by the awareness no value is left behind, at its end.

Psychology: Psychology is reasoning in patterns. To learn about a behavior, or tendency, then spot it in the wild and understand its workings is the essence of the dissection process that is this field. To map these amorphous, initially alien patterns into knowledge, profiling and visualizing are critical tools that shape not only the information, but the process of learning itself and how one defines ideas and gives form to the unknown.

Programming: Creation, iteration, refinement, better, maintain, extend, restart.
Programming is systems manipulation. To absorb, transform, mold code into practical, functional projects. If the solution for a problem exists, learn about it, use and maneuver it as needed. If the solution for a problem does not exist, create it. Information is the programmer's power, and it must be applied and curated for knowledge. The digital is the world of ideas. You are what you think, so think well. The best code is based on time-proven frameworks, practical and simple.
